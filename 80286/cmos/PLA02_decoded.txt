NMOS/CMOS 80286 PLA02 - IU Control PLA

The IU Control PLA sequences instruction decoding. It identifies opcode and
ModR/M formats, then steps through any displacement or immediate fields.

15 inputs, 11 outputs, 121 terms: 101 used (000-100), 20 unused (101-120).
Outputs O0..O5 carry decode controls; O6..O10 feed back as the next state.

State codes and byte values are hexadecimal unless shown as binary.

Inputs
------

Field   | Source input pairs          | Meaning
--------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------------------------
E       | I0                          | 0 = normal opcode, 1 = 0F extended opcode
Q4..Q0  | I1 I3 I5 I7 I9              | Current state, in input order
D       | I13                         | Direct disp16: mod=00, r/m=110 (proposed)
B7..B0  | I11 I2 I4 I6 I8 I10 I12 I14 | Byte from the code queue

B holds the opcode or ModR/M byte, depending on the state. 
D appears to give the expected ModR/M displacement sizes.

State feedback
--------------

Output | Input | State bit
-------+-------+----------
O10    | I1    | Q4
O9     | I3    | Q3
O8     | I5    | Q2
O7     | I7    | Q1
O6     | I9    | Q0

F names the same state in output-bit order:

    Current F = Q0 Q1 Q2 Q3 Q4
    Next F    = O6 O7 O8 O9 O10

The five bits above are written most significant first. For example,
F=16 is binary 10110, so Q4..Q0=01101. F is a state code.

Outputs
-------

Bit     | Alias | Meaning during opcode decode
--------+-------+-------------------------------------------------------------
O0      | DONE  | Opcode/ModR/M decoding complete; trailing data may remain
O1      | W     | Opcode width-bit class
O2      | REG   | Opcode contains a general-register number
O3      | SEG   | Segment-register PUSH/POP
O4      | ALU   | ALU/register-operation class, including PUSH/POP r16
O5      | SEQ   | Control-transfer/special-sequencing class (provisional)
O6..O10 | F     | Next state

DONE does not mean the instruction is fully assembled. For MOV AX,imm16,
DONE asserts on the opcode, before either immediate byte is processed.
O0..O5 are zero throughout the literal states.

Opcode and ModR/M states
------------------------
F=00 and F=01 decode the same way. Prefix identity and the 0F escape
must be stored separately; F=01 only keeps opcode decoding active.

F  | Q4..Q0 | BYTE / OPERATION                             | OPCODE EXAMPLES                             
---+--------+----------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------
00 | 00000  | Opcode                                       | Start of an instruction
01 | 10000  | Opcode after prefix/escape                   | Same decode as F=00
02 | 01000  | Byte unary group; TEST has imm8              | F6
03 | 11000  | Word unary group; TEST has imm16             | F7
10 | 00001  | Ordinary ModR/M, no immediate                | 00-03, 88-8B, D8-DF, etc.
11 | 10001  | ModR/M plus imm8                             | 80, 82, C0-C1, C6
12 | 01001  | ModR/M plus sign-extended imm8               | 6B, 83
13 | 11001  | ModR/M plus imm16                            | 69, 81, C7
1C | 00111  | Shift/rotate by 1 or CL                      | D0-D3
1D | 10111  | FE/FF group                                  | FE-FF

In the ModR/M states, mod and D select the displacement size.
F6/F7 /0-/1 request a TEST immediate; /2-/7 do not.
FE/FF /2-/5 also set O5. These format rules include invalid encodings.
The ModR/M descriptions use E=0; state 10 also behaves the same at E=1.

Trailing fields
---------------
When DONE=1, the next F describes the fields still to be read.
The first field is usually a displacement or address. ENTER uses it
for the frame size; far CALL/JMP use it for the offset.

  O8=0: no trailing fields in the opcode/ModR/M results.
  O8=1: trailing fields remain:
    O6=0: first field present; O7=0 for a word, O7=1 for a signed byte.
    O6=1: first field absent.
    O9 O10: 00 = no second field, 01 = imm8,
            10 = imm16, 11 = sign-extended imm8.

NEXT F | FIELDS REMAINING                            
-------+---------------------------------------------
00     | none
04     | first word
0C     | first signed byte
15     | imm8
16     | imm16
17     | sign-extended imm8
05     | first word + imm8
06     | first word + imm16
07     | first word + sign-extended imm8
0D     | first signed byte + imm8
0E     | first signed byte + imm16
0F     | first signed byte + sign-extended imm8

Literal states
--------------
A literal is an instruction field such as a displacement or immediate.
In these states, only the next F is generated. B, E and D have no effect.
Every literal sequence returns to 00. State 14 is never generated;
if entered, it also returns to 00.

F  | NEXT F | CURRENT OPERATION                                               
---+--------+-----------------------------------------------------------------
04 | 08     | First word low byte
05 | 09     | First word low byte; pending imm8
06 | 0A     | First word low byte; pending imm16
07 | 0B     | First word low byte; pending signed imm8
08 | 00     | First word high byte
09 | 15     | First word high byte; pending imm8
0A | 16     | First word high byte; pending imm16
0B | 17     | First word high byte; pending signed imm8
0C | 18     | First signed byte
0D | 19     | First signed byte; pending imm8
0E | 1A     | First signed byte; pending imm16
0F | 1B     | First signed byte; pending signed imm8
14 | 00     | Unused; never generated
15 | 00     | Immediate byte
16 | 1E     | Immediate word low byte
17 | 1F     | Signed immediate byte
18 | 00     | First-literal sign extension
19 | 15     | First-literal sign extension; pending imm8
1A | 16     | First-literal sign extension; pending imm16
1B | 17     | First-literal sign extension; pending signed imm8
1E | 00     | Immediate word high byte
1F | 00     | Immediate sign extension

The extra phase after a signed byte appears to generate its high byte
by sign extension. The Intel patent shows a sign-extension unit.

Hardware experiments show 1 cycle per state tranistion, with sign extension
taking an extra cycle.

Example sequences
-----------------
For MOV AX,imm16: 00 decodes B8, 16 handles the low immediate byte,
1E handles the high byte, then 00 decodes the next opcode.

INSTRUCTION              | STATE SEQUENCE                          
-------------------------+-----------------------------------------
MOV AX,imm16             | 00 -> 16 -> 1E -> 00
ENTER                    | 00 -> 05 -> 09 -> 15 -> 00
CALL far immediate       | 00 -> 06 -> 0A -> 16 -> 1E -> 00
80 /0 with disp8         | 00 -> 11 -> 0D -> 19 -> 15 -> 00
F7 /0, register          | 00 -> 03 -> 16 -> 1E -> 00
F7 /6, register          | 00 -> 03 -> 00

ModR/M displacement decode
--------------------------
Ordinary ModR/M state F=10, Q=00001; E=0.
D=(mod=00 and r/m=110). The reg field does not affect displacement size.

MOD | R/M       | TERMS       | OUTPUT      | DISPLACEMENT        
----+-----------+-------------+-------------+---------------------
00  | other     | 095         | 10110000000 | none
00  | 110       | 095 097     | 10110000100 | disp16
01  | any       | 000 095     | 10110001100 | disp8
10  | any       | 095 098     | 10110000100 | disp16
11  | any       | 095         | 10110000000 | none (register)

Term table
----------

Each row is one physical term, numbered by source column from left to right.
All matching terms contribute: OR their output bits to obtain O0..O10.

MATCH is ordered: E Q4Q3Q2Q1Q0 D B7B6B5B4B3B2B1B0
  0/1 = required input value; ? = either value.
  In the source matrix, a connection in the first row of an input pair
  matches 0; the second row matches 1; neither connection means ?.

OR lists O0..O10 from left to right. SIGNALS names the set bits.
D8/DISP name O7/O8 in the ModR/M displacement terms.
A mask in a comment contains only that term's F bits. 
Matching masks must be OR'd together.

OPCODES / MODRM / STATE lists opcode bytes or current states and conditions.
Ranges are inclusive; 0F marks extended opcodes. 

F= always means CURRENT F.

The MATCH column gives the full condition, including any byte restrictions.
Some terms serve both ModR/M and literal states; comments describe their
main use. A term match does not imply that an opcode encoding is valid.
Blank TERM cells continue the row above.
